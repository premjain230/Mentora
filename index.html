Built around how memory actually works

Reading a chapter again feels productive, but it's a weak way to learn. Learning Allied uses methods with decades of research behind them.

Socratic tutor

The tutor asks what you think before it explains. Working out an answer yourself helps it stick.

Generation effect, Slamecka & Graf, 1978

Spaced review

Revision is scheduled for just before you'd forget a topic, so you review less often and keep more.

Forgetting curve, Ebbinghaus, 1885

Active recall

Quizzes and questions make you pull answers from memory, which strengthens it more than rereading.

Retrieval practice, Roediger & Butler, 2011

Memory strength

See how well you remember each concept and which ones are fading, so you know what to revise today.

Confidence tracking

Compare how sure you felt with how you actually did. Knowing the gap helps you study the right things.

Metacognition, Flavell, 1979

Prerequisites first

Concepts are linked to the basics they depend on, so you don't build advanced topics on shaky ground.

Plan your syllabus, practise with quizzes, and get help in the same place, whether you're preparing for JEE, NEET or your Board exams.
